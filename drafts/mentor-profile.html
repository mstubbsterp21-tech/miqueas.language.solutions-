About Me

My path into interpreting started with people, not a résumé.

My connection to ASL and the Deaf community developed through direct relationships and community involvement before interpreting became my profession. Those experiences shaped the way I understand language: not as a collection of signs or words, but as something connected to people, culture, context, identity, and access.

I began working professionally as a freelance ASL/English interpreter in 2018. Since then, I have worked across educational, medical, community, and remote settings. One of the biggest shifts in my development has been learning that strong interpreting is not simply about producing a polished target message. It requires processing, judgment, self-awareness, preparation, and the willingness to examine your own work honestly.

That is also what draws me to mentorship: helping another interpreter move beyond “Was that good or bad?” and toward “What happened here, why did it happen, and what can I do with that information?”

Areas of Focus

What we may work on together

These are areas where my experience and mentoring interests are strongest. The focus of a mentorship should still be driven by the mentee's actual goals and needs.

01

ASL → English Voicing

Message equivalence, register, natural English output, clarity, processing, and decisions that affect the final message.

02

English → ASL

Conceptual accuracy, visual organization, meaning, language choices, and moving away from overly form-based output.

03

Processing & Fluency

Lag time, pacing, cognitive processing, message integrity, repair strategies, and identifying patterns behind breakdowns.

04

Educational Interpreting

Classroom decision-making, access, interpreting skill development, and growth connected to the EIPA framework.

05

Professional Judgment

Ethical thinking, role boundaries, interpreting dynamics, preparation, and reflective analysis of professional choices.

06

Goal Development

Turning “I want to get better” into specific, measurable practice goals with reflection and accountability built in.

Why I Mentor

Mentorship should be collaborative—not performative.

I do not want a mentee spending the session trying to prove that they are already good enough. I want us to be able to examine the messy parts of the work, identify patterns, ask better questions, and create a practical path forward together.

What Mentorship Means to Me

Feedback should lead somewhere.

“Work on accuracy” or “improve your ASL” is too broad to be useful by itself. I want feedback to become observable goals, deliberate practice, reflection, and eventually changes that show up in real interpreting work.

Logistics & Practical Expectations

A clear structure, with room to adjust.

1

Initial fit conversation

We start by talking about your interpreting background, what you want to work on, what kind of feedback helps you learn, and whether my mentoring approach fits what you need.

2

Typical session

A typical starting point is a 60-minute one-on-one virtual session. We may use interpreting samples, discussion, self-analysis, targeted exercises, or goal review.

3

Flexible cadence

Mentorship may be short-term and goal-specific or continue over multiple sessions. Frequency can be adjusted based on your goals, availability, and what gives you enough time to practice between sessions.

Available Formats

What I currently have capacity for

My primary format is individual mentorship because it allows us to focus closely on one interpreter's goals and work.

  • One-on-one virtual mentorship — primary format
  • In-person sessions — considered by arrangement and location
  • Small-group or hybrid work — considered when the goals and logistics make sense
  • Session structure can be more fixed or more flexible depending on the goal
What I Need From Mentees

You do not need to arrive perfect.

I do ask that you participate actively in the process and communicate honestly about what is and is not helping you learn.

  • Come willing to reflect—not only defend the final product
  • Bring questions, work samples, challenges, and uncertainty
  • Be open to specific, respectful feedback
  • Practice between sessions when we identify a meaningful next step
  • Tell me when a strategy or approach is not working for you
How to Connect

Email me with a little about where you are in your interpreting journey and what you want help developing. From there, we can talk about your goals and decide whether the mentorship feels like a useful fit for both of us. Fit matters more to me than simply filling a slot.
