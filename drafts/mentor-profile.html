About Me

Interpreting has always been about people first.

I have worked professionally as a freelance ASL/English interpreter since 2018. My experience has included educational, medical, community, remote/VRI, and other real-world settings where communication cannot be reduced to simply replacing one language with another.

My professional journey has taught me to pay attention to meaning, context, culture, processing, relationships, and the decisions interpreters make in real time. I continue to treat interpreting as an evolving practice that requires reflection, feedback, accountability, and continued learning.

Why I Mentor

Mentorship should feel collaborative—not evaluative.

I know how valuable it is to have someone help you slow down, look closely at your work, and translate broad feedback into something specific enough to practice. I do not see mentorship as one person having all the answers.

I see it as a structured partnership: we identify what is happening, explore why it may be happening, test strategies, and build a plan together. I want mentees to be challenged without feeling judged and to leave each session knowing what they are working on next—and why it matters.

What Mentorship Looks Like

Practical structure. Room to adapt.

1

Start with the goal

We identify what you want to improve, what is currently happening in your work, and what success would actually look like.

2

Look at the work

We may use interpretation samples, self-analysis, discussion, targeted exercises, or real professional scenarios.

3

Build the next step

We turn observations into specific practice, reflection, and SMART goals that can be tracked between sessions.

What I Ask From Mentees

Come ready to participate in your own growth.

You do not need to show up perfect. You do need to show up willing to engage with the process.

  • Be open to specific, respectful feedback.
  • Bring questions, samples, challenges, and uncertainty.
  • Reflect on your own decision-making—not only the final product.
  • Communicate when a goal, strategy, or learning approach is not working.
  • Follow through on the practice we agree is meaningful for your goals.
Fit Matters

The first conversation is about whether this makes sense for both of us.

I would rather build a mentorship relationship that is genuinely useful than fill a slot. In your first message, tell me where you are in your interpreting journey, what you want to improve, and what you hope mentorship will help you accomplish.

  • What skill or situation is giving you the most difficulty right now?
  • What kind of feedback helps you learn best?
  • Are you working toward a specific assessment, setting, or professional goal?
Let's Connect
