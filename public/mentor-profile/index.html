My connection to ASL and the Deaf community began through direct relationships and community involvement before interpreting became my profession. Those experiences shaped how I understand language, culture, communication access, and the responsibility interpreters carry in their work.

I have seven years of professional ASL/English interpreting experience across educational, medical, community, and remote settings. Much of my development has come through real-world experience, reflection, feedback, and mentorship.

Why I Mentor

Useful feedback should give an interpreter something clear to work on.

I want mentees to move beyond broad feedback such as “work on accuracy” or “improve your ASL.” Together, we can look at what happened, why it may have happened, and what specific practice, reflection, or adjustment can support growth.

I do not see mentorship as a one-way process. The mentee's goals, experiences, questions, and learning needs should help shape the work we do together.

Community Involvement

I participate in a worldwide Bible education work focused on making Bible education accessible in ASL for the Deaf community. As part of that work, I offer free Bible lessons in ASL.

Mentorship Structure

Initial Conversation

We begin by discussing your experience, goals, current challenges, and what you hope to gain from mentorship.

Sessions

Sessions are typically about 60 minutes and may include interpreting samples, self-analysis, discussion, exercises, or goal review.

Frequency

Mentorship can be short-term or ongoing. Frequency depends on the goal and the amount of practice needed between sessions.

What I Ask of Mentees

  • Be open to reflection and feedback.
  • Bring questions, goals, or work you want to examine.
  • Participate actively in identifying next steps.
  • Practice between sessions when it supports the goal.

How to Connect

Send me a short introduction with your current interpreting experience, what you want to work on, and what you hope to gain from mentorship.

From there, we can have an initial conversation and decide whether my mentoring approach is a good fit for what you need.
