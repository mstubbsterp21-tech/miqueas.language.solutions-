About Me

01

I started learning ASL at age 12, and my language roots run deep in the Deaf community of Central Florida. I did not begin interpreting professionally until 2019, when I was living in Baltimore, Maryland.

Living in Maryland gave me access to a much larger Deaf community in the Washington, D.C. and Gallaudet area. Being around many different native ASL users exposed me to a wider range of signing styles and language experiences, which helped shape my interpreting development over time.

My path has not been perfectly linear. I gained a lot of my development through real-world interpreting, making mistakes, reflecting on them, and working with an interpreter mentor who helped me sharpen both my interpreting skills and my ethical decision-making.

Why I Mentor

04

My own growth as an interpreter was shaped significantly by mentorship.

I made mistakes, gained real-world experience, and had a mentor who helped me strengthen both my interpreting skills and my ethical decision-making. That experience is a major reason I want to develop as a mentor for newer interpreters.

I see mentorship as collaborative and practical. Useful feedback should help a mentee understand what happened, why it may have happened, and what specific practice, reflection, or next step can support growth.

What Mentorship Can Look Like

05

Initial Conversation

We start by talking about your experience, goals, current challenges, and what you hope to gain from mentorship.

Sessions

A typical session is approximately 60 minutes and may include interpreting samples, discussion, self-analysis, exercises, or goal review.

Frequency & Format

Primarily one-on-one virtual mentorship, with in-person, small-group, or hybrid options by arrangement when appropriate.

What I Need From Mentees

06
  • Be open to reflection and feedback.
  • Bring questions, challenges, or interpreting samples.
  • Participate actively in identifying your goals.
  • Practice between sessions when we identify a useful next step.
  • Communicate when an approach is not helping you learn.
  • Be willing to examine both interpreting skills and decision-making.

How to Connect

07

Send me a short introduction with your current interpreting experience, what you want to work on, and what you hope to gain from mentorship.

From there, we can have an initial conversation and decide whether my mentoring approach is a good fit for what you need.
