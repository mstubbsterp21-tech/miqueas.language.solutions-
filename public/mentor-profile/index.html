About Me

I was born and raised in Central Florida, and my journey into American Sign Language began at the grassroots level. I learned ASL directly from Deaf people, through real conversations, relationships, and involvement in the Deaf community. That foundation gave me an appreciation for the language and culture long before interpreting became my profession.

I've spent the past seven years working as an ASL/English interpreter in educational, medical, community, and video remote settings. My path has combined hands-on experience with formal interpreter education at the Community College of Baltimore County, independent study, and continued professional development. Along the way, I earned a 3.9 on the EIPA performance assessment and passed the CASLI Generalist Knowledge Exam.

Today, I'm also the founder of Miqueas Language Solutions, where I work to connect people through accessible communication. My background has taught me to value both the practical demands of interpreting and the people whose communication we support. I bring that perspective to every professional relationship, including mentorship.

Why I Mentor

Professional growth shouldn't depend on knowing where to look for help.

Throughout my interpreting career, I've noticed that many interpreters want to improve but aren't always sure where to find reliable resources or how to turn them into a realistic professional development plan. I mentor because I want to help bridge that gap.

Mentoring with me means having access to a resource library as well as someone who can help you identify your goals, choose useful tools, reflect on your work, and follow through on practical next steps. I'll encourage you and recognize your strengths, while also challenging you to build the skills and judgment your work requires.

Certification is a worthwhile goal, and I encourage interpreters to pursue it. But my focus goes beyond exam preparation. I want mentees to develop the competence and confidence to work effectively in settings they're qualified to accept, whether they're certified yet or not.

Mentorship Structure

Initial Conversation

We begin by discussing your experience, goals, current challenges, and what you hope to gain from mentorship.

Sessions

Sessions are typically about 60 minutes and may include interpreting samples, self-analysis, discussion, exercises, or goal review.

Frequency

Mentorship can be short-term or ongoing. Frequency depends on the goal and the amount of practice needed between sessions.

What I Ask of Mentees

  • Be open to reflection and feedback.
  • Bring questions, goals, or work you want to examine.
  • Participate actively in identifying next steps.
  • Practice between sessions when it supports the goal.

Who Can Apply

This mentorship opportunity is open to interpreting students, recent ITP/IPP graduates, working interpreters, and those returning to the profession. We'll focus on the skills and professional goals you want to strengthen.

All sessions are live and virtual. Participation is subject to availability and fit with the practicum.
