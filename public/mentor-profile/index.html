About Me

My connection to ASL and the Deaf community began through direct relationships and community involvement before interpreting became my profession. Those experiences shaped how I understand language, culture, communication access, and the responsibility interpreters carry in their work.

I began working professionally as a freelance ASL/English interpreter in 2018. My experience has included educational, medical, community, and remote interpreting settings.

Over time, I have learned that interpreting development is not only about producing a better final message. It also requires understanding our processing, decision-making, preparation, language choices, and patterns.

Why I Mentor

I value mentorship because useful feedback should lead to something an interpreter can actually work on.

Rather than stopping at broad feedback such as “work on accuracy” or “improve your ASL,” I want to help mentees identify what happened, why it may have happened, and what specific practice or reflection can support growth.

I see mentorship as a collaborative process. The mentee's goals, experiences, and learning needs should help shape the work we do together.

Mentorship Structure

Initial Conversation

We begin by discussing your experience, goals, current challenges, and what you hope to gain from mentorship.

Sessions

A typical session is approximately 60 minutes and may include interpreting samples, discussion, self-analysis, exercises, or goal review.

Frequency

Mentorship can be short-term or ongoing. Frequency can be adjusted based on the goal and the amount of practice needed between sessions.

Available Formats

  • One-on-one virtual mentorship
  • In-person mentorship by arrangement
  • Small-group or hybrid mentorship when appropriate

What I Need From Mentees

  • Be open to reflection and feedback.
  • Bring questions, challenges, interpreting samples, or areas you want to examine.
  • Participate actively in identifying your goals.
  • Practice between sessions when we identify a useful next step.
  • Communicate when an approach is not helping you learn.

How to Connect

Send me a short introduction with your current interpreting experience, what you want to work on, and what you hope to gain from mentorship.

From there, we can have an initial conversation and decide whether my mentoring approach is a good fit for what you need.
